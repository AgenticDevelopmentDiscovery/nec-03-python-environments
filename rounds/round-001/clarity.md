# Clarity & Exposition — Round 1

**Recommendation:** needs revision
04-conclusion silently drops the C1/C2/C4 downstream-application content both `topic.md` and its own spine say it delivers.

## Strengths
- The WHAT → WHY → HOW → WHAT ELSE arc actually holds together on a shared example: the SciPy/NumPy version-bump failure named in `02-motivation.prose.md` is the exact scenario reproduced on purpose in `03-content.prose.md`'s demo ("bump one dependency without pinning it and rerun — watch the score shift... the exact failure `02-motivation` described"). The reader isn't asked to generalize from a fresh toy example at each stage.
- `03-content.prose.md`'s tool-walkthrough sections (`venv`, `uv`) keep prose to a framing sentence and let the command-annotated figures carry the specifics, rather than spelling out each flag in sentence form. That discipline is what keeps a dense HOW section readable at slide length.

## Weaknesses
- `sections/04-conclusion.prose.md` — "What you can do now" contains only two generic capability sentences ("You can create an isolated Python environment... You can explain why pinning isn't enough..."). It never mentions C1, C2, or C4, even though `04-conclusion.concepts.md`'s Decisions log states plainly: "Folded the C1/C2/C4 connections into 'What you can do now' as concrete downstream use" — and `topic.md` §Shape promises the same thing. The spine and the proposal both describe content that the shipped prose does not contain.
- `sections/03-content.prose.md` § Demo: prove it with the symbolic-regression substrate — the section that is supposed to *prove* the tutorial's central claim narrates it instead of showing it: "Build the same environment two ways... Generate a lockfile, delete the environment entirely, rebuild it from the lock alone, and rerun... Then bump one dependency without pinning it and rerun." No command appears anywhere in the unit, in contrast to every walkthrough section before it, which pairs a short frame sentence with a figure of actual commands.
- `sections/01-context.prose.md` § What this tutorial covers — "The difference between pinning a dependency and locking the full resolved tree — what actually makes a run reproducible." is a sentence fragment: there is no main verb once it's read as its own sentence (as its capital letter and trailing period present it). It's one of five hard-break fragments strung together in place of a roadmap paragraph.
- `sections/03-content.prose.md` § How it works: spec to lockfile — opens with "Four things are easy to confuse: the **interpreter**, the **package set**, the **spec**, and the **lockfile**," but only spec ("A spec is loose, written by hand...") and lockfile ("resolves into a lockfile: one exact build...") get a defining clause across the two "How it works" units. "Interpreter" is used only as an actor ("Hand it to the interpreter") and is never itself defined as one of the four things.
