# Visual & Multi-Register Exposition — Round 1

**Recommendation:** minor polish
Figures are purposeful and well-integrated throughout; the issues are one likely slide-overflow that was never rendered-checked, one register-dependent slide, and one real coverage gap on the tutorial's central proof.

## Slide overflow
- `sections/01-context.prose.md` § Where it came from — overflows because: too many ideas (estimate). The unit opens with the venv/`site-packages` origin story, carries `figures/isolation-before-after.svg` — at viewBox height 300, the tallest figure in the whole document — then closes with a second, unrelated origin story (`conda`'s and `uv`'s). Every other figure in this project that shipped at ~300px needed to shrink to ~190–290px before it fit next to even one short sentence on each side (per the `03-content.concepts.md` log: "viewBox height ≲ 210 for a figure that shares a slide with two sentences of prose"); this one carries ~72 words of surrounding prose across two separate ideas and, unlike every other figure, its concepts.md entry never logs a check against a rendered deck. Worth a direct look at `output/slides.pdf`.
- `sections/03-content.prose.md` § Demo: prove it with the symbolic-regression substrate — overflows because: genuinely dense (low-confidence estimate). No figure, and three sentences narrate roughly eight sequential actions (build twice, confirm match, lock, delete, rebuild, rerun, compare, bump, rerun, compare again) as a single unbroken paragraph. It may well fit the frame at metropolis's default size — but even if it clears the frame edge, it is the densest text-only unit in the document; worth confirming it doesn't just barely fit.

## Strengths
- `figures/env-mental-model.svg` and `figures/env-mental-model-2.svg` (document + slides + site): the split into "spec → lockfile" and "lockfile → environment" reuses the lockfile box as the hinge between both figures, so the pair reads as one continuous pipeline in the document while each half still stands alone as its own slide — a genuine solution to the register conflict between long-form flow and single-slide isolation.
- `figures/isolation-before-after.svg` and `figures/pin-vs-lock-tree.svg` (all three registers): both use the same before/after grammar — dashed divider, problem labeled in orange/red, resolution in green — giving the deck a consistent visual language for "here's the gap, here's it closed" a reader can learn once and reapply.
- `figures/drift-chain.svg` (all three registers): the green-check/red-X "assumed vs. actual" pairing compresses a causal-misdiagnosis argument that would otherwise take a full paragraph to land, into something readable at a glance — this is the figure that most clearly earns its place by making visible what the prose alone leaves abstract.
- `figures/venv-create.svg` and `figures/uv-lockfile.svg` (slides + document): parallel annotated-terminal structure across two consecutive slides makes the venv-vs-uv comparison easy to hold in mind without the reader having to reread either one.
- Every figure in the document is referenced at the exact point the prose introduces the concept, and every caption is self-contained enough to teach a reader who has only the figure (no caption depends on prose above or below it to make sense).

## Weaknesses
- `sections/01-context.prose.md` — "Where it came from" asks one figure and one heading to carry two unrelated origin stories (venv's, then conda/uv's); see Slide overflow above.
- `sections/03-content.prose.md` — "`uv`: fast installs and lockfiles" opens with "`uv` does the same three jobs faster," a callback to the previous slide's four venv commands. A reader who lands on this slide in the deck, or on this heading from a site search result, has no way to resolve what "the same three jobs" refers to — and the count itself is slightly off (venv's walkthrough shows four commands, not three), which will read as an error rather than a callback to someone who never saw the venv slide.
- `sections/03-content.prose.md` — "Demo" and "Pitfalls" are the only two `##` units in this section without a figure, and the section's own `03-content.concepts.md` (Open questions) flags exactly this as worth an outside read. Pitfalls is a short three-item list and doesn't need one. Demo is different: it is the tutorial's central proof (per `topic.md`, "the destroy-and-rebuild demo that proves a lockfile actually reproduces the environment") and currently has no visual reinforcement at all — the one relationship the whole document has been building toward is asserted only in prose.

## Actionable
1. `sections/01-context.prose.md` § Where it came from — either split the unit in two (venv's origin as its own `##`, conda/uv's origin folded into "What this is" where the tools are first named), or shrink `figures/isolation-before-after.svg` toward the ~210px ceiling this project has already established elsewhere and cut the conda/uv closing sentences down to the single clause "What this is" doesn't already need.
   (*why:* repairs the slide register — this is the one figure+prose combination in the document that was never confirmed against a rendered deck, and it is currently larger, in both figure size and word count, than every combination the team already had to fix)
2. `sections/03-content.prose.md` § `uv`: fast installs and lockfiles — replace "does the same three jobs faster" with something that names the jobs inline (e.g. "creates a sandbox, installs, and locks — what `venv`+`pip` took four commands to do, faster") or drop the callback entirely and let `figures/uv-lockfile.svg`'s three labeled steps carry it.
   (*why:* repairs the presentation and website registers — both require this unit to stand alone without the venv slide in view)
3. `sections/03-content.prose.md` § Demo: prove it with the symbolic-regression substrate — add one compact outcome figure, reusing `drift-chain.svg`'s check/X grammar: two build paths (`venv`+`pip`, `uv`) converging on a single "fitness score ✓" node; a second arrow from "delete → rebuild from lock" back to that same ✓ node; a third branch from "bump one dependency, unpinned" to a "✗ different score" node. Not a step-flow of the six actions (the section's concepts.md already and correctly rejected that as restating an already-ordered narrative) — a convergence/divergence diagram showing that two different build paths and one destroy-and-rebuild all land on the identical score, while one unpinned change doesn't.
   (*why:* repairs all three registers at once — this is the claim the entire document has been building toward, it is currently prose-only, and it would also rebalance a section that is otherwise front-loaded with figures in its first five units and bare in its last two)
