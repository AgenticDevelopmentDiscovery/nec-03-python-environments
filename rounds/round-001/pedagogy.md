# Pedagogical Readiness — Round 1

**Recommendation:** needs revision
The mental model and the `venv`/`uv` walkthroughs are genuinely teachable, but the document's one proof moment — the destroy-and-rebuild demo — isn't executable from the page, and a capability the proposal promises for the close never made it into the prose.

**Audience read against:** Grad students in the agentic-development course who can already write/run Python and have used `pip`, but have never made a virtual environment, generated a lockfile, or had to reproduce a result on a second machine — i.e., comfortable with Python, new to environment/dependency management specifically.

## Strengths
- The spec → lockfile → environment mental model (`03-content`, first two `##` units) is genuinely well-paced: two short figures each carrying one relationship, prose doing only what the figure can't, and it reads as a single line of reasoning rather than four facts in a trenchcoat.
- The `venv` and `uv` walkthroughs are real follow-along material, not description-of-a-tool: `figures/venv-create.svg` and `figures/uv-lockfile.svg` both contain the literal, runnable commands (`python -m venv .venv`, `pip freeze > requirements-lock.txt`, `uv lock`, etc.) annotated with what each does — a reader could type these and get the stated result.
- `02-motivation`'s failure case is concrete and correctly scoped to this audience: a silent SciPy/NumPy bump shifting a fitted score, drawn from the course's own substrate rather than a generic "a dependency updated," which is exactly what keeps it from reading as invented.
- `metadata.yaml`'s `audience` field is specific enough to actually judge against — named prior knowledge, named gaps — which is rarer than it should be and makes the rest of this review possible.

## Weaknesses
- `sections/03-content.prose.md` § Demo: prove it with the symbolic-regression substrate — this is the tutorial's one proof step (per `topic.md`: "the destroy-and-rebuild demo that proves a lockfile actually reproduces the environment"), and it is pure narration: "build the same environment two ways... generate a lockfile, delete the environment entirely, rebuild it... bump one dependency without pinning it." No command, no transcript, no figure — `topic.md` explicitly posed this as an open question ("does the demo need a figure, or does the CLI transcript carry it on its own?") and the shipped section answers with neither. Every other hands-on claim in this document (`venv` create, `uv lock`) is backed by literal commands the reader can run; the one claim the whole tutorial is built to prove is the one left abstract. A reader at the declared level cannot actually perform this demo from what's on the page.
- `sections/04-conclusion.prose.md` § What you can do now — `topic.md`'s Shape section promises this unit folds in "how the capability is used downstream: C1's reproducible-repo challenge, C2's comparable leaderboard, C4's generate→evaluate→select loop," and `03-content.concepts.md`'s Decisions log states this was done ("Folded the C1/C2/C4 connections into 'What you can do now'"). It wasn't: the shipped paragraph names only the environment/lockfile capability itself, with no downstream connection at all. This is the exact promise-not-delivery pattern the spine is supposed to make visible — here the spine itself is a step ahead of the prose it describes.
- `sections/03-content.prose.md` § Pitfalls — `topic.md`'s scope names `PYTHONHASHSEED` explicitly as part of "the second axis of reproducibility." The shipped Pitfalls bullet defines seeding only via `random.seed(42)`; `PYTHONHASHSEED` never appears anywhere in the tutorial. A reader following only what's on the page walks away thinking library-level seeding is the whole story, which is a narrower claim than the proposal scoped.
