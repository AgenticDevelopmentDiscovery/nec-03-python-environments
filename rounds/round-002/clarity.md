# Clarity & Exposition — Round 2

**Recommendation:** needs revision
core vocabulary ("pinning") is used before it's defined, and the downstream payoff both `topic.md` and the section's own spine promise never lands in the conclusion.

## Strengths
- The macro through-line holds together: each section opens by reaching back into the last one instead of starting cold — `03-content` opens by naming the "four things easy to confuse" that `01`/`02` left implicit, and `02-motivation` opens by reframing an agent run as an experiment, which is the frame `03-content`'s demo then cashes out. This is a real WHAT→WHY→HOW→WHAT-ELSE arc, not four sections juxtaposed.
- The "figure carries the enumeration, prose carries the point" discipline visible throughout keeps sections tight — e.g. `02-motivation.prose.md`'s drift paragraph is two sentences either side of a figure rather than a paragraph spelling out the whole causal chain in words.
- `03-content.prose.md` § Pitfalls is genuinely economical: three bullets, each a distinct trap (partial pinning, an uncommitted lockfile, seeding vs. locking) rather than the same warning restated three ways.
- `02-motivation.prose.md` § When to reach for it states a checkable boundary rule ("lock what you'll compare, share, or trust later... a throwaway exploration doesn't need it") instead of a blanket mandate — it respects the reader's judgment rather than overclaiming the practice always pays off.

## Weaknesses
- `sections/02-motivation.prose.md` — opens "Pinned environments and lockfiles are what let you reproduce a leaderboard result," and later "The real fault is an unpinned dependency." Neither "pin" nor "pinned" has appeared anywhere before this point: `sections/01-context.prose.md` defines "environment" and "lockfile" but never uses the word, even though `01-context.concepts.md`'s own Claims list it as owed here ("A lockfile is not the same thing as a pin..."). The WHY section leans on a term the WHAT section promised to deliver and didn't.
- `sections/04-conclusion.prose.md` § What you can do now — two sentences, both about mechanics (create/lock/reconstruct, pin vs. transitive). `04-conclusion.concepts.md`'s Decisions claims "Folded the C1/C2/C4 connections into 'What you can do now' as concrete downstream use," and `topic.md`'s Shape makes the same promise for this section by name. Neither C1, C2, C4, "leaderboard," nor any downstream application appears anywhere in the file. The spine describes a decision that was never actually carried out in the prose.
- `sections/01-context.prose.md` — "What this is" and "What this tutorial covers" are both terse bulleted fragments, while "Where it came from" between them, and every other section in the document, is written in connected prose. The reader's first exposure to the document's voice is list → prose → list within one section, which reads as unedited notes bracketing a finished paragraph rather than one considered piece of exposition.
- `sections/03-content.prose.md` § How it works: spec to lockfile — "Four things are easy to confuse: the interpreter, the package set, the spec, and the lockfile" sets up a four-way distinction, but "package set" never gets its own defining clause; it is simply used again two sections later ("installs that exact package set"). Two of the four named terms (interpreter, package set) are left to inference while the other two get a figure each.

(*why:* right now the tutorial's closing section recaps mechanics but drops the "why this mattered for you specifically" the plan promised — the strongest close available is currently unused.)
3. `sections/01-context.prose.md` §§ What this is / What this tutorial covers — rewrite both as 2–4 sentence prose paragraphs, matching "Where it came from" and the rest of the document's register. (*why:* the opening section is the reader's first impression of the document's voice; three register changes in one section reads as assembled notes, not as the considered order-of-argument the rest of the document delivers.)
4. `sections/03-content.prose.md` § How it works: spec to lockfile — add a short defining clause for "package set" at first mention (e.g. "the package set — what's actually installed"). (*why:* the sentence asks the reader to track four terms through two consecutive `##` units; leaving one undefined weakens the distinction the two-part mental model is built on.)
